01

FLAVOR · 맛

Jang & seasoning

Korean flavor is usually built in layers rather than with one all-purpose sauce. Establish salt and savory depth, add aromatics and heat, then finish with a small amount of fragrant oil or seeds.

Doenjang된장

A robust fermented soybean paste used in stews, soups, vegetable seasoning, and ssamjang.

Gochujang고추장

A fermented red pepper paste with heat, sweetness, and body for stir-fries, sauces, and mixed dishes.

Ganjang간장

Soup soy sauce seasons pale broths; brewed and jin soy sauces are versatile for braising and stir-frying.

Chamgireum참기름

Roasted sesame oil used mainly for aroma. Add a little near the end rather than using it as a high-heat oil.

A simple flavor sequence

  1. 1
    Choose the base

    Begin with soy sauce, doenjang, or salt. Add less than you think you need.

  2. 2
    Build aroma

    Garlic, scallion, and sometimes ginger shape the direction of a dish.

  3. 3
    Adjust heat and sweetness

    Gochugaru adds direct chile flavor; gochujang adds heat, sweetness, and thickness.

  4. 4
    Finish for fragrance

    A few drops of sesame oil and a pinch of toasted sesame seeds are often enough.

Your first purchase

Start small.
Taste as you cook.

  • Check the product name and ingredient list, not just the package color.
  • Soy sauces differ in salinity, color, and purpose. Follow the type named in the recipe when possible.
  • Follow package storage directions and use a clean, dry spoon for pastes.
  • Gochujang and doenjang vary by brand, so make final adjustments at the end.

Taste the difference The fastest way to understand jang is to cook with each one.

02

FERMENTATION · 발효

Kimchi & banchan

Kimchi is not one dish but a broad family shaped by vegetables, season, region, and household tradition. Banchan connect rice to the rest of the table by balancing flavor, temperature, and texture.

Baechu-kimchi배추김치

The familiar napa cabbage kimchi, seasoned and fermented for a deep, lively flavor.

Kkakdugi깍두기

Cubed radish kimchi prized for its crisp, juicy bite.

Baek-kimchi백김치

White kimchi made with little or no red pepper, so it is mild and refreshing.

Oi-sobagi오이소박이

Cucumbers filled with chives and seasoning, often enjoyed while fresh and crunchy.

What banchan do

Balance seasoning

Salty, tangy, or spicy bites make plain rice more expressive without overwhelming it.

Change the texture

Tender namul, crisp kimchi, and a browned jeon keep a meal varied.

Carry the season

Vegetables can be blanched, dressed, stir-fried, pickled, or braised for everyday use.

Make sharing easy

Rice and soup are usually individual; many banchan sit in the center for everyone.

Good to know

Kimchi is not always
spicy—or vegetarian.

  • Baek-kimchi and dongchimi use little or no red pepper.
  • Traditional kimchi may contain fish sauce or salted seafood. Check labels when buying vegan kimchi.
  • As kimchi ages, it becomes more sour and aromatic. Well-ripened kimchi is ideal for stew and fried rice.
  • Portion refrigerated banchan as needed and avoid leaving them at room temperature for long periods.
KIMJANG · 김장

Kimjang—the late-autumn practice of making and sharing a large supply of kimchi—was inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2013. It carries knowledge of seasonality, family practice, and community sharing.

Cook with ripe kimchi Heat brings a new savory depth to kimchi that has become pleasantly sour.

03

THE TABLE · 식탁

How a meal works

A familiar Korean table centers on rice with soup or stew, kimchi, and a few side dishes. The elements balance one another—but an everyday meal does not need to include every piece.

YOUR BOWLBap · 밥

The anchor of the meal, ready to receive the table’s stronger seasonings.

YOUR BOWLGuk · 국

Guk and tang are broth-forward; jjigae is usually more concentrated and substantial.

SHAREDKimchi · 김치

Fermented acidity and crunch reset the palate between bites.

SHAREDBanchan · 반찬

Namul, grilled, braised, or pan-fried dishes bring different techniques and seasonal foods.

OPTIONALMain · 주요리

Meat, fish, or tofu may be shared from the center or portioned onto individual plates.

Three easy tables

KEEP IT SIMPLE

Bibimbap + kimchi

Rice, vegetables, protein, and seasoning meet in one bowl, so a large spread is unnecessary.

EVERYDAY HOME MEAL

Rice + doenjang jjigae + namul + kimchi

Warm broth, tender vegetables, and crisp kimchi balance temperature and texture.

WHEN SHARING

Rice + soup + bulgogi + two banchan

Place one generous main dish in the center and enjoy it with individual rice and soup.

At the table

Sharing matters more
than perfect rules.

  • In a traditional setting, rice sits on the left and soup on the right.
  • The spoon is commonly used for rice and soup; chopsticks are used for banchan.
  • Alternate small bites and use serving utensils when they are provided.
  • At formal gatherings, younger diners may wait for the eldest person to begin. Modern family meals are often relaxed.
ONE BOWL CAN BE A WHOLE MEAL

Bibimbap, gukbap, and naengmyeon show that Korean food does not require a crowded table. Start with one complete dish and add only what helps.

Build your first table Begin with an approachable bowl, then add one or two banchan.

04

SHOPPING · 장보기

Ingredient guide

You do not need to buy an entire Korean pantry at once. Choose two or three recipes, then start with the ingredients they share.

THE FLAVOR BASE

Soy sauce · doenjang · gochujang

Buy smaller containers first and check storage and allergen information.

AROMA & HEAT

Gochugaru · sesame oil · toasted sesame

Gochugaru comes in different grinds; fragrant sesame oil is best used while fresh.

THE MEAL FOUNDATION

Rice · gim · kimchi

Short- or medium-grain rice suits the table. Kimchi heat and ripeness vary by product.

BROTH & SAVORY DEPTH

Anchovy · kelp · fish sauce

These are optional. Kelp and dried mushrooms can build a satisfying vegetarian broth.

Words to find on the label

양조간장Brewed soy sauce

A versatile choice for dipping, dressing, and stir-frying

국간장Soup soy sauce

Pale but quite salty; use sparingly in soup and namul

고춧가루Gochugaru

Coarse for kimchi and stews; fine grades suit some sauces

참기름Sesame oil

A roasted, fragrant finishing oil

들기름Perilla oil

A distinct nutty oil used for namul, noodles, and more

액젓Fish sauce

Adds salty depth to kimchi, soups, and dressings

Substituting abroad

Match the role,
not just the name.

  • If short-grain rice is unavailable, use medium-grain rice. Long-grain rice needs different water and has a different texture.
  • If soup soy sauce is unavailable, combine a little regular soy sauce and salt—but expect a darker broth and different aroma.
  • Ordinary chile flakes can be much hotter and more bitter than gochugaru, so do not automatically substitute equal amounts.
  • Use lettuce for wraps when perilla leaves are unavailable. Shiso provides aroma, but it is not identical.
Check allergens and dietary needs

Soy sauce, doenjang, and gochujang may contain soy and wheat; seasonings may contain sesame. Kimchi and broth can include fish sauce, salted seafood, or anchovy. For vegan, gluten-free, or allergy-sensitive cooking, check every product’s ingredient and allergen label.
