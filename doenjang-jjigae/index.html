Doenjang Jjigae Recipe

2025-05-11 · KFOOD JOURNAL

Hello everyone! Welcome to Kfood Journal. Today, I’m excited to share with you the dish that never leaves a Korean table—the familiar yet never tiresome doenjang jjigae (soybean paste stew). Though its aroma is strong, its flavor is even stronger. Let’s uncover the secrets behind this deeply savory dish together.

The History of Doenjang Jjigae: A Bowl of Common Wisdom

Doenjang jjigae has been a staple since the Joseon Dynasty, embodying the wisdom of ordinary Koreans. Made from fermented soybeans, doenjang (soybean paste) is rich in protein and has a long shelf life, making it a precious source of nutrition for our ancestors. In times when meat was scarce, doenjang provided that essential umami flavor. The stew also reflects the wisdom of seasonal eating, incorporating different vegetables throughout the year to maintain nutritional balance.

Ingredients (Serves 2)

Main Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons doenjang (soybean paste)
  • 1/2 tablespoon gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes, optional)
  • 1/2 block of tofu
  • 1/4 Korean zucchini (aehobak)
  • 1 medium potato
  • 1/2 onion
  • 1 green chili pepper (for heat adjustment)
  • 1 stalk of green onion
  • 2 shiitake mushrooms (or enoki mushrooms)
  • 10 dried anchovies (for stock)
  • 1 piece of dried kelp (5x5cm, for stock)
  • 3 cups water

Seasonings

  • 1 tablespoon minced garlic
  • 1 teaspoon soup soy sauce (for taste adjustment)
  • 1 teaspoon perilla oil

Step-by-Step Cooking Process

1. Making Anchovy Stock

  1. In a pot, combine 3 cups of water with the anchovies and dried kelp over medium heat for 10 minutes.
  2. Once it starts to boil, remove the kelp, and let the anchovies simmer for another 5 minutes before removing them.

2. Preparing the Vegetables

  1. Cut the tofu into 2cm cubes.
  2. Slice the zucchini into half-moons about 0.5cm thick.
  3. Peel the potato and cut it into 1cm cubes.
  4. Slice the onion.
  5. Slice the green chili at an angle and finely chop the green onion.
  6. Remove the stems from the shiitake mushrooms and slice them 1cm thick.

3. Making the Doenjang Jjigae

  1. In the pot with the prepared stock, add perilla oil and sauté the minced garlic to release its fragrance.
  2. Add the potato and onion, and sauté for 2 minutes.
  3. Add the doenjang, dissolve it well in the broth, then add the gochugaru and sauté briefly.
  4. Add the zucchini and mushrooms and simmer for 2 minutes.
  5. Add the tofu and simmer for another 3 minutes.
  6. Add the green chili and green onion, simmer for 1 more minute, then turn off the heat.
  7. If needed, adjust the saltiness with soup soy sauce.

5 Key Points for Perfect Doenjang Jjigae

  1. Quality Doenjang Makes the Difference: Using traditionally fermented doenjang results in deeper umami flavors.
  2. Use Anchovy and Kelp Stock: This creates a flavor base that’s 10 times deeper than using just water.
  3. Sauté the Doenjang: Briefly sautéing the paste removes any unpleasant fermentation smell and enhances its nutty flavor.
  4. Follow the Order of Ingredients: Start with firm vegetables (like potatoes) and add tofu later to prevent it from breaking apart.
  5. Let It Rest: Allowing the stew to sit for about 5 minutes after cooking helps the flavors meld together beautifully.

Creative Variations: Doenjang Jjigae Transformations

  1. Seafood Doenjang Jjigae: Add clams, shrimp, or mussels for a refreshing ocean flavor.
  2. Kimchi Doenjang Jjigae: Add finely chopped slightly aged kimchi for an extra depth of flavor.
  3. Pork Belly Doenjang Jjigae: Render fat from pork belly before making the stew for added richness.
  4. Mushroom Doenjang Jjigae: Include various mushrooms (oyster, enoki, shiitake, king oyster) for a healthy flavor boost.
  5. Bean Sprout Doenjang Jjigae: Add bean sprouts for a crisp texture and refreshing taste.

Serving Tips

  • Serve in a hot stone pot or earthenware to keep it steaming hot longer.
  • Pair with rice and seaweed for a complete meal.
  • Try the “doenjang jjigae risotto” by mixing rice directly into your stew.
  • Serve extra green chilies on the side for those who enjoy more heat.
  • Just before serving, add a drop of sesame oil to enhance the nutty flavor.

A Bowl of Comfort: Doenjang Jjigae

Doenjang jjigae is more than just food. The aroma that greets you when you return home after a long day brings a sense of comfort and stability. It’s no coincidence that this dish tops the list of foods most missed by Korean students studying abroad. Through this single bowl, we experience a mother’s touch, a taste of home, and the essence of Korean sentiment. Fill your day with warmth through a bowl of doenjang jjigae tonight or tomorrow morning.
