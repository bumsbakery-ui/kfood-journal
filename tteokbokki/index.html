Tteokbokki: Sweet and Spicy Temptation, Korea’s Eternal Street Food Romance

2025-05-12 · KFOOD JOURNAL

Hello everyone! Welcome to Kfood Journal. Today, we’re diving into ‘tteokbokki,’ a dish beloved by all Koreans and an intriguing challenge for foreigners. Let’s uncover the secrets behind these chewy rice cakes dancing in red sauce—a flavor so addictive you’ll never want to escape its spell!

The History of Tteokbokki: From Palace to Streets, Master of Transformation

Did you know that tteokbokki was originally a royal court dish called ‘gungjung tteokbokki’ during the Joseon Dynasty? This original version, seasoned with soy sauce and filled with meat and vegetables, was nothing like today’s spicy version. In the late 1950s, someone accidentally mixed rice cakes with gochujang (red pepper paste) and fell in love with the taste, giving birth to the spicy tteokbokki we know today. From snack shops to street carts, it has evolved into Korea’s national snack with countless variations.

Ingredients (Serves 2)

Main Ingredients

  • 400g rice cakes (garaetteok for tteokbokki)
  • 4 pieces fish cake (odeng/eomuk)
  • 1/2 onion
  • 1 stalk green onion
  • 2 boiled eggs (optional)
  • 50g sweet potato noodles (dangmyeon, optional)
  • 2 cups water

Sauce

  • 3 tablespoons gochujang (Korean red pepper paste)
  • 2 tablespoons gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes)
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon minced garlic
  • 1 tablespoon oligosaccharide or corn syrup
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil

Step-by-Step Cooking Process

1. Preparing the Ingredients

  1. Rinse the rice cakes under running water and drain.
  2. Cut the fish cakes into appropriate sizes.
  3. Slice the onion and cut the green onion diagonally into 5cm lengths.
  4. If using sweet potato noodles, soak them in hot water for 5 minutes, then drain.

2. Making the Sauce

  1. In a bowl, mix gochujang, gochugaru, sugar, soy sauce, minced garlic, and oligosaccharide.
  2. Add 3 tablespoons of water and stir until smooth.

3. Cooking the Tteokbokki

  1. Pour 2 cups of water into a pot and bring to a boil over medium heat.
  2. Once boiling, add the prepared sauce and mix well.
  3. When the sauce begins to boil, add the rice cakes and simmer for 5 minutes over medium heat.
  4. As the rice cakes begin to soften, add the fish cakes and onions and simmer for another 2-3 minutes.
  5. Finally, add the green onions and sweet potato noodles (if using) and cook for 1 more minute.
  6. Turn off the heat, drizzle with sesame oil, and top with halved boiled eggs.

5 Key Secrets for Fantastic Tteokbokki

  1. The Rice Cake Condition Is Crucial: If your rice cakes are too dry, soak them in water for about 10 minutes; if they’re too soft, reduce cooking time.
  2. Dissolve the Sauce in Water Before Adding Rice Cakes: Ensure the sauce is thoroughly mixed with water before adding rice cakes for even flavor distribution.
  3. Cook with the Lid Off: Cooking with the lid on can make the rice cakes too soft. Keep it uncovered to maintain the right consistency.
  4. Sweetness Balance Is Key: If it’s too spicy, add a bit more sugar or oligosaccharide to neutralize the heat.
  5. Finish with a Drop of Sesame Oil: A drop of sesame oil after turning off the heat adds a wonderful nutty aroma.

Creative Variations: Tteokbokki Transformations

  1. Cheese Tteokbokki: Top with plenty of mozzarella cheese at the end and melt for a fantastic combination of creamy cheese and spicy rice cakes.
  2. Rose Tteokbokki: Add heavy cream or milk to the sauce for a creamy rose sauce version.
  3. Jjajang Tteokbokki: Use black bean paste (chunjang) instead of gochujang for a different flavor profile.
  4. Army Stew Tteokbokki: Add sausages, bacon, and kimchi for a hearty budae-jjigae style meal.
  5. Seafood Tteokbokki: Incorporate squid, shrimp, and clams for a deeper, refreshing flavor.

Serving Tips

  • Tteokbokki is best enjoyed hot.
  • Pair with fish cake skewers, seaweed rolls, or tempura for a more satisfying experience.
  • Try ‘tteokbokki fried rice’ by mixing leftover sauce with rice.
  • Serve with fresh vegetables like cherry tomatoes or cucumber to balance the spiciness.
  • Sweet drinks (like sikhye or sujeonggwa) can help temper the spiciness.

Red Temptation, Chewy Happiness

Do you remember the taste of tteokbokki shared with a friend under one umbrella at a street cart on a rainy day? Or that special flavor enjoyed with friends after exams? Tteokbokki is more than just food—it’s a soul food filled with Koreans’ memories and daily life. The spicy-sweet broth, the chewy texture of rice cakes, and the softness of fish cakes… a single bowl of tteokbokki contains so many of our stories. Why not experience this simple happiness with homemade tteokbokki tonight or on the next rainy day?
