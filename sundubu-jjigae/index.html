Sundubu-jjigae: Silky Soft Tofu Embraced in Spicy Broth

2025-05-18 · KFOOD JOURNAL

Hello everyone! This is Kfood Journal. Today, we’re introducing Sundubu-jjigae, a beloved Korean stew that perfectly balances the silky smoothness of soft tofu with the bold spiciness of Korean chili flakes. This comforting dish warms both body and soul, especially during cold winter months.

The History of Sundubu-jjigae: From Home Kitchens to Global Sensation

Sundubu-jjigae has its roots in traditional Korean home cooking, where freshly made soft tofu was celebrated for its delicate texture and nutritional benefits. The dish gained tremendous popularity in the 1980s and 1990s, with specialized restaurants called “Sundubu-jip” emerging throughout Korea. It has since become a staple in Korean communities abroad, particularly in Los Angeles’ Koreatown. Recently, with the global rise of Korean culture, Sundubu-jjigae has captivated international palates as one of Korea’s most beloved culinary exports.

Ingredients (Serves 2)

Main Ingredients

  • 1 block (about 400g) of soft tofu
  • 100g ground beef (optional)
  • 100g shellfish (clams, small mussels)
  • 50g shrimp (optional)
  • 1 egg
  • 50g enoki mushrooms
  • 1 green chili pepper
  • 1/2 green onion
  • 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
  • 2 cups water or kelp stock

Seasoning

  • 2 tablespoons Korean red chili flakes (gochugaru)
  • 1 tablespoon soup soy sauce (gukganjang)
  • 1 tablespoon minced garlic
  • Salt to taste
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil
  • Toasted sesame seeds for garnish
  • 1 teaspoon fish sauce (optional)

Preparation Method

  1. Preparation Steps
    1. Soak the shellfish in salted water to remove sand.
    2. Chop the green onion finely and slice the green chili pepper diagonally.
    3. Trim the base of the enoki mushrooms and separate them.
    4. Cut the soft tofu into large chunks.
  2. Cooking the Stew
    1. Heat vegetable oil in a pot, then stir-fry the ground beef until browned. Add garlic and sauté until fragrant.
    2. Add the red chili flakes and stir quickly to prevent burning, then pour in the water or kelp stock.
    3. Add the soup soy sauce and fish sauce, adjusting the seasoning to taste.
    4. Add the cleaned shellfish and shrimp, bringing the broth to a boil.
    5. When the shells begin to open, carefully add the soft tofu in large pieces, followed by the enoki mushrooms.
    6. Add the green chili pepper and green onion, then bring to a final boil.
    7. Crack an egg on top, drizzle with sesame oil, and sprinkle with sesame seeds to finish.
  3. Serving
    1. Serve immediately while bubbling hot, allowing the egg to cook to a soft consistency at the table.
    2. Enjoy with a bowl of rice.

5 Golden Secrets for Perfect Sundubu-jjigae

  1. Use Fresh Soft Tofu: If possible, use homemade or freshly made soft tofu. The silky texture is the highlight of this dish.
  2. Leverage Shellfish Umami: Clams and mussels add natural depth and seafood flavor to the broth. Ensure they’re properly cleaned to avoid any sand.
  3. Use a Stone Pot: A traditional ttukbaegi (stone pot) or earthenware pot retains heat longer, keeping the stew bubbling hot throughout the meal.
  4. Timing the Red Pepper Flakes: Briefly toast the gochugaru in oil for a nutty aroma, but be careful not to burn it as it can develop a bitter taste.
  5. Add the Egg Last: The egg is best enjoyed when still soft and partially cooked. Add it just before serving.

Sundubu-jjigae Variations

  • Seafood Sundubu-jjigae: Enhanced with various seafood (squid, mussels, oysters) for a richer oceanic flavor
  • Kimchi Sundubu-jjigae: Incorporates well-fermented kimchi for added depth and complexity
  • Mushroom Sundubu-jjigae: Features diverse mushrooms for enhanced texture and nutritional value
  • Doenjang Sundubu-jjigae: Uses Korean soybean paste instead of chili for a milder, earthier flavor
  • Canned Sundubu: Utilizes convenient pre-packaged Sundubu stew base available at Korean grocery stores

Serving Suggestions

Sundubu-jjigae is traditionally served in a hot stone pot or earthenware bowl, brought bubbling to the table. It’s typically enjoyed with rice and accompanied by banchan (side dishes) such as kimchi or pickled vegetables. For a more substantial meal, pair it with a simple Korean pancake like pajeon (green onion pancake) or kimchijeon (kimchi pancake). On cold days, pairing with soju or makgeolli creates a warming combination that’s perfect for chasing away the chill. The level of spiciness can be adjusted by varying the amount of chili flakes or adding fresh chili peppers.

A Bowl of Warmth, Sundubu-jjigae’s Embrace

The harmonious meeting of silky soft tofu and spicy broth offers a moment of pure culinary pleasure, a brief respite from the busyness of everyday life. On a cold winter day, what could be more comforting than sharing a steaming pot of Sundubu-jjigae with those you love?
